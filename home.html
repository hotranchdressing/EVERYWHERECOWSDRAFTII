Valorization, n. The act or process of giving, assigning, or enhancing value.

Valorization is the transformation of an unwanted byproduct into a useful commodity. Cattle derivatives are surprisingly multi-valent, appearing in everything from building materials to cosmetics. These transformations make cattle an invisible yet pervasive stakeholder in the products, surfaces, and architectures that shape our everyday life.


Rendering, n. A translation. A representation. Also, the process of extracting fat from flesh.

If valorization makes cattle inevitable in everyday life, it simultaneously vanishes the animal. In cities such as New York, where products made from cattle deriviates circulate widely but livestock are rarely seen, the separation is especially stark.

From Donna Haraway’s “When Species Meet” (University of Minnesota Press, 2008): “responsibility is a relationship crafted in intra-action through which entities, subjects and objects, come into being.” Visualizing our dependence on industrial cattle production is an interpretation of what Haraway calls “ecologies of significant others,” by reaching for the multi-species messmates who find themselves at the table.
